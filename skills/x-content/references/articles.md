# X Article Content Intelligence

Use this reference for X Articles and scoped Article tasks. Keep Article strategy subordinate to verified evidence, owner provenance, the active account voice, and any governing Growth OS decisions.

This workflow is a clean conceptual adaptation informed by practitioner Article workflows, including `codejunkie99/x-article-skills`. Treat those external methods as optional heuristics, not account evidence, causal growth laws, or a creator persona to imitate.

## When Article is the right format

Choose Article when most of these are true:

- the contribution is durable rather than only reacting to a moment;
- the reader benefit needs substantially more depth than a Thread can comfortably carry;
- evidence, examples, steps, code, prompts, screenshots, diagrams, or artifacts materially improve the contribution;
- one coherent reader promise can organize the piece;
- there is enough grounded material to avoid padding;
- the result can become durable owned proof or useful researched synthesis.

Do not choose Article merely because:

- the topic is trending;
- the source post is long;
- a long format appears more authoritative;
- a Thread underperformed;
- a thumbnail can be generated;
- another creator reports strong Article reach.

If one idea fits in a strong Original or short Thread, use the smaller format.

## Classify the contribution before drafting

Choose the strongest truthful evidence class.

### Owned proof

Verified first-person build, test, implementation, or result evidence belonging to the account owner.

First-person performance/result language is allowed only to the extent supported by the evidence.

### Owned observation

Verified first-person observation without a stronger result claim.

Do not silently upgrade observation into tested causality.

### Researched synthesis

A thesis built from inspected sources. Speak as analysis/research, not as personal product use.

### Documented implementation reading

A conclusion based on inspected code, docs, architecture, or public artifacts without claiming execution.

### Proposal / design

A suggested architecture, method, or experiment that has not been represented as tested.

### Social / taste essay

A supported judgment or perspective that does not require invented empirical authority.

Topic relevance, access to a repository, or familiarity with a tool is not proof that Hamza used or benchmarked it.

## Build the Article brief

Before full drafting, establish:

- intended reader;
- reader question/problem;
- contribution/evidence type;
- one central thesis;
- strongest evidence;
- material uncertainty;
- strongest objection or counterpoint when relevant;
- what the reader should understand or be able to do afterward.

If owner/project material exists, distinguish verified project identity from verified outcomes. Knowing that a project exists does not justify invented production maturity, performance, failures, usage, or benchmark results.

A researched Article is valid when firsthand evidence is absent. Change the voice of the claim, not the truth standard.

## Angle selection

Prefer an angle with a real information gain for the reader.

Strong angles may include:

- a concrete implementation method;
- an architecture or workflow decision;
- a result that can be reproduced or inspected;
- a trade-off that changes tool choice;
- a failure mode with a practical consequence;
- a comparison with an explicit decision boundary;
- a researched synthesis across several sources;
- an opinion with enough evidence/context to be useful;
- a resource/blueprint that saves readers real work.

Do not choose an angle solely because it sounds viral. The angle must survive after the trend disappears.

## Promise integrity

Treat the Article package as one promise expressed across:

`title -> opening -> body -> visual -> reusable artifacts -> launch copy`

The elements do not need identical wording. They must point to the same reader value and stay within the same evidence boundary.

Before polishing prose, test:

1. What does the title make the reader expect?
2. Does the first screen confirm that expectation?
3. Does the outline actually deliver it?
4. Does the visual explain or evidence the same mechanism/result?
5. If files/prompts/code are promised, do they exist and help reproduce the value?
6. Does the launch post promise no more than the Article delivers?

A catchy title that overstates the body is a defect, not a growth optimization.

## Titles

Choose the title family that matches the contribution.

Possible families:

- outcome/how-to;
- supported judgment;
- technical question;
- experiment/result when actually run;
- resource/blueprint;
- personal process when owner provenance supports it;
- technical essay/synthesis.

Do not force a how-to title onto a judgment essay or research synthesis.

Prefer titles that make the reader benefit and object legible without resorting to generic superlatives or curiosity withholding.

Use a number only when the number is real and materially defines the promise.

## Opening

Establish the payoff early enough that the intended technical reader knows why to continue.

No fixed number of lines is mandatory.

A compact summary/TLDR is optional. Use it when it meaningfully reduces reader work; omit it when it duplicates a clear opening.

Do not mechanically use:

- a broad dramatic claim;
- an artificial contrarian hook;
- a personal anecdote with no provenance;
- a fixed five-to-seven-line abstract;
- a symmetrical analyst contrast.

The entry point can be conversational, technical, opinionated, or evidence-first as long as the promise becomes clear.

## Body

Use only the depth the promise requires.

Possible ingredients:

- explanation;
- steps;
- code;
- prompts;
- screenshots;
- diagrams;
- source comparisons;
- benchmark/result tables;
- failure cases;
- decision criteria;
- reproducible examples.

Constraints:

- one central argument;
- concrete nouns and observable behavior;
- source claims separated from Hamza's interpretation;
- no invented first-person proof;
- no padding to reach a target length;
- no mandatory analyst-style conclusion;
- no automatic section count;
- stop when the promise is fulfilled.

Long technical sections may remain long when consequence or reproducibility requires them. Voice fidelity does not mean artificial casual compression.

## Evidence and reproducibility

For consequential factual claims, preserve enough provenance for the reader or operator to inspect them.

When the Article promises a workflow or implementation, ask what a competent reader would need to reproduce it:

- exact tool/model/version when material;
- prerequisites;
- commands or configuration when material;
- inputs and outputs;
- failure/edge conditions;
- what was measured and how;
- which parts are proposed rather than tested;
- source links or artifact references.

Do not manufacture reproducibility theater. If the piece is an essay rather than a tutorial, it does not need fake steps or a repository.

## Visual brief

`x-content` owns what the visual should communicate, not image-generation mechanics.

A useful brief may specify:

- central relationship/workflow/result;
- intended aspect ratio/dimensions when known;
- what text must remain readable in feed preview;
- the dominant visual object;
- required labels/data;
- which references supply which facts or visual properties;
- preservation constraints for later edits;
- claims the visual must not overstate.

A diagram, screenshot, code image, or thumbnail is optional. Use the visual form that reduces reader work.

Do not import another creator's palette, thumbnail formula, or SVG as Hamza's default visual identity.

Use the image tool when actual generation/editing is requested.

## Reusable package

When the Article promises reusable or reproducible material, define the smallest complete handoff.

Possible contents:

- Article copy;
- source/evidence notes;
- reusable prompts;
- diagrams;
- screenshots with appropriate provenance;
- relevant project subset;
- example inputs/outputs;
- README or reproduction instructions.

Do not create files merely to imitate a successful creator's package. Every artifact must serve the reader promise.

`x-content` may define the package. Git/GitHub tools own file creation in repositories, staging, commits, pushes, visibility, and remote verification.

## Article review contract

Prioritize material defects over stylistic preference.

Review these questions:

1. Does the Article fulfill the title/opening promise?
2. Is the central contribution actually additive rather than a paraphrase of sources?
3. Which claims are firsthand, sourced, inferred, proposed, or unverified?
4. Does any sentence imply use/testing/results not supported by owner evidence?
5. Can the intended reader understand the entry point without decoding unnecessary abstractions?
6. Does every major section advance the promise?
7. If reproducibility is promised, where would a reader get stuck?
8. Does the visual, if any, depict the same mechanism/result without overstatement?
9. Do promised reusable files actually exist and match the described task?
10. Does the final language sound like Hamza rather than institutional analyst prose or an external creator's voice?
11. Does the Article stop after fulfilling the promise rather than adding a generic conclusion/CTA?

Classify feedback as:

- **Required correction** — factual, provenance, promise, usability, or material voice defect.
- **Optional preference** — non-blocking stylistic choice.

Do not use a model self-score such as 10/10 as the stopping condition.

## Launch copy

Generate launch copy only after the Article promise is coherent, unless the user explicitly asks for hook exploration earlier.

The launch post should:

- state a concrete payoff;
- use only claims supported by the Article/evidence;
- preserve Hamza's current voice;
- not summarize the entire Article;
- not import another creator's reach numbers or personal authority;
- not force a standard CTA or "give this to your agent" formula;
- respect any downstream Growth OS route/experiment decision.

Treat launch copy as its own X content object. It still needs the normal publication authority path.

## Anti-drift rules

### External creator voice capture

Do not make the Article lowercase, aphoristic, hook-first, CTA-ended, or visually "normie-max" merely because an external workflow uses that style.

Use external workflow ideas for process only. The account voice remains authoritative.

### Analyst memo morphology

Do not convert a good technical idea into stacked abstractions and polished symmetric contrasts.

If a sentence sounds like a consulting slide rather than a builder explaining something, rewrite it more naturally without weakening the technical point.

### First-person inflation

Never infer `I tested`, `I built`, `we use`, `in production`, or performance claims from topic relevance, repository access, or project identity.

### Formula lock-in

Do not require every Article to contain a how-to title, TLDR, diagram, numbered steps, fixed opening length, GitHub repo, or CTA.

Each treatment must earn its place from the content object and reader promise.

### Overproduction

A request for one title, hook, review, outline, or visual brief does not authorize a full production workflow.

## Handoffs

- **Live X context:** use `agent-browser`; do not duplicate its mechanics here.
- **Growth OS:** preserve candidate provenance, route, experiment, approval, scheduling, and publication authority.
- **Image generation/editing:** use the image tool with the visual brief.
- **Git/GitHub work:** use the governing repository/connector workflow.
- **X Article publication:** do not assume an Article route or browser write path exists merely because `x-content` can draft the Article.

The content layer can be complete while publication remains manual or unsupported.
