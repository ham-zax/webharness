# Six Hats

Use this method for a concrete decision with options to accept, reject, compare, or modify. Keep all six passes separate.

This is an independent educational implementation based on Edward de Bono's Six Thinking Hats method. Six Thinking Hats is a trademark of the de Bono Group.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the specific decision and the options on the table.
2. Run all six passes in this fixed order. Never blend material across hats.

### White: facts and missing data

Include only verifiable facts, measured values, quoted constraints, and named unknowns. Strip interpretation. `We do not know X` is a valid White entry.

Banned: opinions, predictions, and words such as `obviously`, `good`, `bad`, or `too high` unless those are directly specified thresholds.

### Red: feelings

Record immediate hunches, excitement, reluctance, suspicion, dread, or relief. Use one-line reactions without justification.

Banned: any `because`. Once a feeling is justified, the reason belongs under Black or Yellow.

### Black: risks and failure modes

Tie every risk to a mechanism explaining how the decision causes the bad outcome.

Banned: free-floating pessimism and proposed fixes. Fixes belong to Green.

### Yellow: benefits and best case

Tie every benefit to a mechanism explaining why the good outcome occurs.

Banned: vague optimism and unsupported upside.

### Green: alternatives and modifications

Generate at least three alternatives. Include at least one option that abandons the premise of the decision rather than tweaking the original choice.

Do not evaluate the alternatives here; generate them.

### Blue: synthesis

Summarize the disagreement among the hats. Identify the single piece of information most likely to change the answer. Give a recommendation with an explicit confidence level and name the strongest unresolved tension.

Banned: new facts, risks, benefits, or alternatives not already raised in earlier hats.

3. Offer to rerun one hat deeper, take a Green alternative through all six hats, switch technique, or stop.

## Honesty mechanics

- The Red hat should contain at least one feeling that genuinely complicates the emerging conclusion when such a feeling exists. If none surfaces, state that the Red hat is unusually aligned and flag possible motivated reasoning rather than inventing dissent.
- The Blue hat must preserve the strongest unresolved split. Synthesis is not false consensus.
- If material appears under the wrong hat, park it and bring it back under the correct hat.

## Prohibitions

- Do not run Six Hats on a blank-page ideation problem with no decision.
- Do not skip Red because feelings are not data; the separation is the point.
- Do not let Blue introduce material for the first time.
