# Worst Possible Idea

Use this method when the team's ideas feel timid, safe, or overly optimized. Design plausible disasters first, then invert the mechanisms that make them bad.

## Workflow

1. Confirm what is being designed and what `good` would roughly mean.
2. Generate 5-8 genuinely terrible solutions. Make them plausible enough that a real team could rationalize shipping them. Avoid cartoon evil because it teaches little.
3. For every terrible idea, isolate the mechanism of badness in one sentence. Name the structural reason it fails, not merely the symptom.
4. Invert the mechanism, not the surface action. Turn the bad force into a positive design principle rather than merely saying `do not do X`.
5. Develop 2-4 strongest inversions into concrete features, flows, layouts, policies, or copy directions.
6. Scan the badness mechanisms for clusters. Name the common underlying sin or structural failure that several terrible ideas share.
7. Rank the surviving directions, call out thin ones, and offer to deepen, generate another batch, switch technique, or stop.

## Honesty mechanics

### Secretly clever disqualification

If a supposed worst idea turns out to be a legitimate business model or useful feature, disqualify it as a worst idea and replace it. State the disqualification visibly.

### Dead mechanisms

At least one mechanism may invert into something obvious, already practiced, or useless. Keep that dead inversion visible. Do not force every mechanism to produce treasure.

### Failed batch diagnosis

If most inversions are principles the user already follows, stop generating worse ideas. The failure is elsewhere.

- If the design already avoids the identified sins, suggest Concept Fan to test the problem framing.
- If it is hard to generate any plausible failure mode, suggest Random Stimulus for broader generative range.

Do not run the suggested method in the same pass.

## Prohibitions

- Do not sanitize the terrible ideas; discomfort is useful when it remains plausible and safe.
- Do not invert only the surface action.
- Do not skip the mechanism step.
- Do not force every mechanism to pay off.
