# Random Stimulus

Use this method when ideas all feel similar and an external association could break the pattern.

## Workflow

1. Confirm a concrete creative target. If unclear, ask one focused question about the problem and hard constraints.
2. Draw 8-12 unrelated stimuli across at least five categories. Include at least two concrete objects and at least one abstraction. Do not place two stimuli from the same category next to each other.
3. For every stimulus, show the chain:
   - stimulus and category
   - 3-5 structural properties
   - force-fit jump to the target
   - concrete direction, or explicit abandonment
4. Apply the redundancy test to every attempt: ask whether the idea could have been reached from the target alone without the stimulus. If yes, the stimulus did no work; abandon it.
5. Stop working one stimulus after two successive force-fit attempts fail the redundancy test. A third attempt may only confirm it is dead.
6. Expect roughly 2-3 abandonments in a batch of 8-12. Do not make every stimulus succeed.
7. Scan the winners and abandonments for a meta-pattern. Name the recurring structural property and the common reason dead stimuli failed.
8. Rank 3-5 sharpest directions, call out weak ones, and offer to deepen, draw a fresh batch, switch technique, or stop.

## When the batch fails

If more than half the stimuli die, do not draw more. Diagnose instead:

- If the target is phrased as a solution rather than a problem, suggest Concept Fan next.
- If a fixed constraint blocks every force-fit, suggest Provocation next.

Do not run the suggested method in the same pass.

## Stimulus pool

Choose unpredictably and spread the categories.

### Plants and nature

Oak rings, bamboo grove, mangrove roots, dandelion seed, lichen, pine cone opening with heat, river delta, glacier, tide pool, hurricane eye, fog, volcano, canyon, archipelago.

### Animals and biology

Octopus, hermit crab, beaver dam, salmon run, chameleon, owl, ant trail, bee scout, slime mold, mycorrhizal network, fermentation, gut microbiome.

### Food and household

Sourdough starter, tea steeping, bento box, cast-iron seasoning, leftovers, pickling, mirror, mailbox, junk drawer, ice-cube tray, alarm clock, key ring.

### Tools and transit

Pulley, lever, loom, typewriter, vending machine, compass, microscope, crane, conveyor belt, lighthouse beam, ferry, bicycle, escalator, container ship.

### Art, music, and stories

Jazz improvisation, choir, metronome, rest in music, origami fold, kintsugi, mosaic, collage, unreliable narrator, footnote, plot twist, choose-your-own-adventure.

### Spaces and rituals

Courtyard, fire escape, train concourse, secret passage, museum gallery, wedding, vigil, tea ceremony, parade, ribbon cutting, procession, housewarming.

### Games and roles

Poker bluff, relay handoff, escape room, chess opening, hide-and-seek, lighthouse keeper, librarian, cartographer, watchmaker, concierge, simultaneous interpreter.

### Time, perception, and abstractions

Hourglass, circadian rhythm, eclipse cycle, tree ring, heartbeat, nostalgia, blind spot, white noise, momentum, entropy, threshold, critical mass, feedback loop, network effect, signal versus noise, opportunity cost, recursion, paradox.

### Curiosities

Russian doll, Trojan horse, Mobius strip, ship of Theseus, prisoner's dilemma, time capsule, message in a bottle, unopened gift, dead-letter office.

## Quality rules

- Favor structural properties such as cyclical, layered, branching, self-reinforcing, ephemeral, gated, swarming, or reversible.
- Do not sanitize weird directions merely because they are impractical at first glance.
- Do not repeat stimuli within the same conversation.
- Keep a batch under about 15 stimuli.
