# Forced Analogy (Structural Transplant)

Use this method when the solution works but feels derivative, or when a distant system may already solve the same structural problem.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the system to reshape and where it jams.
2. Rewrite the problem as one domain-neutral sentence containing actors, flows, and the bottleneck. Remove industry vocabulary and solution language.
3. Choose 3-5 distant domains whose structures rhyme with that sentence. Include at least one biological, one operational, and one social or cultural domain.
4. For each domain:
   - Map roles explicitly from target to source domain.
   - Ask: `What does this domain do about the bottleneck that we do not?`
   - Transfer 1-2 mechanisms, not imagery, into concrete target ideas.
5. Apply the one-forced-role rule. One metaphorical leap is acceptable; if a second forced correspondence is needed to keep the analogy alive, abandon it visibly.
6. Drop surface analogies that share vocabulary but not structure.
7. Scan successful and failed mappings for a meta-pattern. Name the structural treatment that kept transferring.
8. Rank the strongest transplants and offer next moves.

## Domain pool

### Biological

- Immune system: distinguishes self from non-self, remembers threats, and mounts graded responses without one controller.
- Ant colony: global routes emerge from local rules and strengthen with use.
- Mycorrhizal network: redistributes resources from surplus to deficit across independent participants.
- Slime mold: reinforces efficient paths and abandons waste without central planning.
- Cell apoptosis: components self-destruct so the whole remains healthy.

### Operational

- Air-traffic control: many autonomous actors share one congested channel under strict collision constraints.
- Restaurant brigade: unpredictable orders flow through specialized stations and synchronize at the handoff.
- Emergency triage: demand exceeds capacity, so work is ranked by urgency and continuously re-ranked.
- Container shipping: diverse contents become interchangeable through a standard interface.
- Warehouse pick path: optimize travel between work rather than the work itself.

### Social and cultural

- Jazz ensemble: tight shared structure plus local freedom and responsive coordination.
- Jury deliberation: independent views must become one binding decision under a constraint.
- Potluck: abundance emerges from uncoordinated contributions without central assignment.
- Guild masterwork: access is gated by produced proof of competence.
- Barn raising: a large task is completed through reciprocal community labor rather than direct payment.

### Historical and systems

- Lighthouse network: independent reliable stations collectively make a coastline navigable.
- Relay post system: speed comes from handoffs and fresh riders, not one heroic carrier.
- Cartography expedition: each exploration frontier becomes the next starting point.
- Power grid: supply and demand must balance continuously with pressure valves for overload.
- Fermentation: design the initial conditions, then let a self-propagating process run.
- Forest fire succession: periodic destruction clears accumulated risk and enables renewal.

## Honesty mechanics

Expect at least one mapping to fail. If every domain needs a second forced role, rewrite the structure sentence once. If it still fails:

- If the sentence keeps describing a solution instead of a shape, suggest Concept Fan.
- If the structure is clear but nothing maps, suggest Random Stimulus.

Do not run the suggested method in the same pass.

## Prohibitions

- Do not choose near-neighbor industries simply because the words overlap.
- Do not transfer aesthetics such as color, vibe, or metaphor when the mechanism is what matters.
- Do not use more than five domains in one batch.
