# Concept Fan

Use this method when a current solution may be answering the wrong question or when all options are cosmetic variations of the same branch.

## Workflow

1. Require a current solution or approach as the starting point. If the user has only a blank page, ask for the current approach first.
2. Ask: `What is this a way of doing?` Treat the answer as a broader direction.
3. Ask the same question once more to climb one additional level. Stop after two climbs; a third usually becomes a platitude.
4. At each useful level, fan out 3-4 alternative concepts that also serve the direction above.
5. For the 2-3 most promising alternatives, drop back down into 2-3 concrete implementations each.
6. Mark obvious branches explicitly. Prune any branch that is the original solution wearing a new label.
7. Scan across branches for a structural insight: a hidden shared assumption, a wrong optimization variable, or two apparently distant branches that are the same move.
8. Rank the branches by promise, call out weak ones, and offer to fan wider, drop down deeper, start from another solution, switch technique, or stop.

## Honesty mechanics

Show cosmetic branches being pruned. A fan where every branch survives is usually not divergent enough.

If both climbs become phrases such as `deliver value`, `make users happy`, or `grow the business`, declare the fan failed. Suggest Forced Analogy next because a parallel system may give more traction than further abstraction.

## Prohibitions

- Do not climb more than twice.
- Do not fan more than four concepts per level.
- Do not treat the fan as a commitment tool; it widens the map.
